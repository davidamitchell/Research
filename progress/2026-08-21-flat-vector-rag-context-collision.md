Answers whether context collision in flat-vector Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is solely a context-window limit or a deeper structural failure. Finding: it is two independent mechanisms, not one -- a context-window position/length mechanism (Lost in the Middle, RULER) and a distinct contradiction-resolution mechanism evidenced by Alchourron-Gardenfors-Makinson (AGM) postulate violations (belief inertia, collateral retraction) that persists when context length is held constant. Standard flat-vector mitigations (reranking, compression, source-aware prompting) narrow but do not close the second gap; the one intervention shown to measurably improve multi-hop coherence (Reasoning Tree Guided RAG) does so by introducing an explicit relation scaffold, though a transient query-time scaffold suffices -- a full persisted knowledge graph is not required. Graph advantage is bounded to contradiction-heavy, relationally dense tasks. `confidence: medium` (AGM-Bench is an unreviewed 2026 International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR) submission; no controlled ablation isolates RT-RAG's relational-structure contribution from its retrieval-recall improvement).
