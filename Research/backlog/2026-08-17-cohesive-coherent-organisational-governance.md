# What constitutes cohesive and coherent organisational governance for aligned, high-velocity, low-risk decentralised decision-making in large organisations facing AI-driven change?

## Research Question

What constitutes good cohesive and coherent organisational governance—i.e., the specific configurations, principles, mechanisms, and performance thresholds that reliably produce aligned, high-velocity, low-risk decentralised decision-making while preserving strategic integrity—and how can these be diagnosed, measured, and sustained in large established organisations facing continuous digital and Artificial Intelligence (AI)-driven change?

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Normative criteria for cohesiveness: internal consistency of decision rights, organisational structures, and accountability mechanisms
- Normative criteria for coherence: external fit with strategy, environment, and technology
- Observable markers, leading indicators, and outcome thresholds that distinguish high-performing governance systems from fragmented or contradictory ones
- Diagnostic tools, maturity models, and design principles for assessing and improving governance state
- Sustainability mechanisms: how governance is maintained or regenerated as AI agents, data volumes, and environmental turbulence increase
- Evidence from large established organisations (financial services, regulated industries, major enterprises)

**Out of scope:**
- Start-up or greenfield governance design (different constraints and starting conditions)
- Pure AI/technical governance (model risk management, data lineage) in isolation from organisational governance
- Narrow compliance-only governance frameworks

**Constraints:** Published empirical literature preferred over advisory/consulting white papers; theoretical frameworks must be grounded in observable organisational behaviour; time horizon 2015–present given pace of digital and AI change

## Context

The prior comprehensive framing of interdependencies among decision governance systems, organisational design/operating models, and multi-level accountability architectures (captured across the split-authority cluster and governance-controls-effectiveness-conditions items) established *how* these three domains interact. This item advances the inquiry by asking *what good looks like* as an integrated system: defining explicit criteria, observable markers, and sustainability mechanisms that organisations can use to assess and improve their current governance state. The question is directly relevant to large organisations navigating AI-agent deployment and decentralisation pressures while preserving regulatory accountability.

## Approach

Decompose the question into the following sub-questions:

1. **Cohesiveness criteria** — What conditions make decision rights, organisational structures, and accountability mechanisms internally consistent? What are the observable signs of inconsistency (governance gaps, authority overlaps, accountability voids)?
2. **Coherence criteria** — How is external fit between governance design and strategic intent, technology capability, and environmental turbulence defined and measured? What frameworks exist for assessing fit?
3. **Performance thresholds** — What quantitative or qualitative thresholds distinguish high-performing governance systems? What does the empirical literature say about velocity, risk, and alignment as measurable outcomes?
4. **Diagnostic tools and maturity models** — What published instruments (maturity models, assessment frameworks, audit approaches) exist for organisational governance diagnosis? How reliable and validated are they?
5. **Sustainability under AI-driven change** — How do organisations regenerate governance coherence as AI agents, automation, and data volumes disrupt established decision structures? What patterns have been observed in early-adopter organisations?
6. **Design principles** — What minimal set of design principles emerges from the evidence as sufficient to produce and sustain cohesive, coherent governance at scale?
