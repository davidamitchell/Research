# Alignment limits from missing shared background

## Research Question

To what extent are artificial-agent alignment strategies constitutively limited by the absence of the shared embodied, evolutionary, developmental, and cultural background that makes human value-laden communication workable, and which non-linguistic mechanisms, if any, show evidence of reconstructing a functional equivalent of that background for artificial agents?

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Strong philosophical, cognitive-science, and anthropological arguments that explicit language and formal systems underdetermine meaning, value, or pragmatic competence.
- Classic alignment arguments on hidden complexity, orthogonality, specification failure, genie/outcome-pump problems, and related goal-transmission limits.
- Substrate questions about embodiment, developmental history, multimodal grounding, metabolic self-maintenance, social learning, and long-horizon cultural co-evolution.
- Historical or cognitive precedents for externalised command, including bicameral-mind arguments, inner speech, auditory verbal hallucinations, and the social origins of self-regulation.
- Empirical evidence from child language acquisition, cross-cultural value transmission, primate social cognition, and communication failure where it materially informs the alignment question.
- Evidence for or against functional substitutes for the missing background in current Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems, including multimodal grounding, multi-agent interaction, and long-run co-adaptation.

**Out of scope:**
- A comprehensive history of phenomenology, theology, or nihilism beyond what directly sharpens the alignment question.
- Vendor-specific alignment product comparisons or near-term implementation recipes for one model family.
- A full survey of all alignment agendas unrelated to communication, specification, grounding, or goal transmission.
- Fresh experiments or benchmark creation in this session.

**Constraints:** (time, source types, access)
- Prioritise primary sources and strong secondary syntheses over commentary where possible.
- Distinguish clearly between claims of impossibility in principle, extreme practical difficulty, and partial but promising workaround mechanisms.
- Use publicly accessible sources, plus prior completed repository items where they directly bear on the question.
- Treat empirical studies as evidence, not illustration, and keep speculative synthesis explicitly marked during later investigation.

## Context

This question informs whether alignment should be framed mainly as a better-specification engineering problem or as a deeper medium-and-substrate problem in which human goals cannot be stably transmitted to systems that do not share the historical, embodied background that makes human communication barely workable even among humans.

## Approach

1. Test the strongest versions of the claim that language and formal representation are radically incomplete for transmitting meaning, values, or context, and identify what kind of "background" each tradition says is doing the real work.
2. Re-read classic alignment and specification-failure arguments through that lens to determine whether they describe contingent engineering gaps or a deeper failure of medium, shared substrate, or world-model inheritance.
3. Examine historical and cognitive cases of externalised directive-following to clarify what it means for a steering signal to remain outside the optimising system rather than becoming reflectively owned.
4. Compare functionalist and substrate-dependent accounts of agency to identify which background-enabling properties look necessary, optional, or replaceable for value-laden communication.
5. Survey empirical and technical evidence on possible substitutes for the missing background, including embodiment, multimodal grounding, multi-agent interaction, developmental learning, and long-run cultural co-adaptation.
6. Assess whether hyperstitional or deterritorialised steering signals generate a functional replacement for missing background or instead intensify externality, instability, and specification drift.
