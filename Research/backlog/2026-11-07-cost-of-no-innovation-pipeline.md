# What is lost when organisations rely only on emergent problem lodging?

## Research Question

Compared with a dedicated innovation pipeline, what is lost in problem coverage, speed of learning, selection quality, and ability to scale improvements when medium-to-large knowledge-work organisations rely solely on emergent problem lodging and have no formal innovation budget?

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Gaps in which problems are surfaced, represented, or selected for attention.
- Differences in learning speed and experiment throughput between emergent and dedicated pipeline approaches.
- Differences in selection quality, including visibility of cross-team value and the effects of unequal voice or local incentives.
- Limits on promotion, coordination, and scaling of local improvements.
- Conditions that mitigate or amplify these losses, grounded in the completed findings from the two preceding research items.

**Out of scope:**
- Treating a dedicated pipeline as universally superior or assuming it is justified regardless of cost.
- Individual ideation techniques.
- Detailed implementation of experiment platforms or pure statistical design of online controlled experiments.

**Constraints:** Do not begin until the two cited research items are complete. Separate evidence-based losses from plausible but unverified risks, and distinguish missing capability from measured performance impact.

## Context

Emergent problem lodging may avoid the overhead of a formal pipeline, but the organisation needs to understand its opportunity costs before deciding whether to rely on it or add structured selection and promotion mechanisms.

## Approach

1. Use the completed emergent-path item (`2026-11-07-emergent-friction-lodging`) to identify the capabilities and safeguards available without a dedicated pipeline.
2. Use the completed dedicated-pipeline item (`2026-11-07-dedicated-innovation-pipeline-value`) to identify the incremental mechanisms and benefits that a formal pipeline may provide.
3. Compare the approaches on four explicit dimensions: problem coverage, speed of learning, quality of selection, and ability to scale improvements.
4. For each claimed loss, distinguish whether it is directly evidenced, inferred from a missing mechanism, or dependent on organisational context.
5. Identify countervailing strengths of emergent lodging and conditions where the absence of a pipeline creates little or no material loss.
6. Produce findings that can feed the subsequent cross-item synthesis into an operating standard.
