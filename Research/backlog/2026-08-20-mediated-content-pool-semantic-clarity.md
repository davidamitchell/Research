# Semantic clarity and conflict resolvability in mediated content pools

## Research Question

What domain-independent methods from knowledge engineering, ontology alignment, inconsistency management, and structured knowledge curation can be applied at the level of a mediated content pool to improve semantic clarity, make latent conflicts easier to surface, and attach explicit resolvability structures that remain useful across downstream execution environments?

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Content-pool-level methods such as semantic normalisation, ontology alignment, conflict detection, invariant extraction, provenance capture, and resolvability annotation
- Mediation mechanisms that shape how pool material is surfaced, indexed, linked, or normalised before any active harness consumes it
- Evaluation criteria for whether pool-level improvements deliver durable leverage across repeated downstream uses
- Comparisons between structural curation patterns that change the pool directly and overlay patterns that preserve raw material while improving clarity and conflict visibility

**Out of scope:**
- Domain-specific rules about what material may or may not be written into the pool
- Techniques that act only inside a transient working context or execution harness
- Policies for how downstream actors should respond once a surfaced conflict is detected

**Constraints:** Treat the content pool as both the accessible material and the mediation layer that exposes it. Prioritise transferable methods with evidence from knowledge graph, ontology, archival, or curated-corpus settings, and distinguish intrinsic pool improvements from benefits that depend on a particular consumer environment.

## Context

This item informs how to make a reusable mediated corpus intrinsically clearer, easier to audit, and more explicitly resolvable before any specific harness or execution environment consumes it.

## Approach

1. Define the target properties of a mediated content pool: semantic clarity, conflict-surfaceability, and explicit resolvability.
2. Catalogue concrete pool-level techniques from ontology alignment, truth maintenance, semantic normalisation, provenance, and curation research.
3. Compare how those techniques expose contradictions, preserve context, and support later resolution without relying on a specific execution harness.
4. Identify evaluation criteria and metrics that show durable leverage across repeated downstream uses of the same pool.
5. Synthesize a transferable method set and decision framework for improving pool quality independent of any one application domain.
