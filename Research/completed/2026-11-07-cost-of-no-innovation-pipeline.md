# What is lost when organisations rely only on emergent problem lodging?

## Research Question

Compared with a dedicated innovation pipeline, what is lost in problem coverage, speed of learning, selection quality, and ability to scale improvements when medium-to-large knowledge-work organisations rely solely on emergent problem lodging and have no formal innovation budget?

## Scope

**In scope:**
- Gaps in which problems are surfaced, represented, or selected for attention.
- Differences in learning speed and experiment throughput between emergent and dedicated pipeline approaches.
- Differences in selection quality, including visibility of cross-team value and the effects of unequal voice or local incentives.
- Limits on promotion, coordination, and scaling of local improvements.
- Conditions that mitigate or amplify these losses, grounded in the completed findings from the two preceding research items.

**Out of scope:**
- Treating a dedicated pipeline as universally superior or assuming it is justified regardless of cost.
- Individual ideation techniques.
- Detailed implementation of experiment platforms or pure statistical design of online controlled experiments.

**Constraints:** Do not begin until the two cited research items are complete. Separate evidence-based losses from plausible but unverified risks, and distinguish missing capability from measured performance impact.

## Context

Emergent problem lodging may avoid the overhead of a formal pipeline, but the organisation needs to understand its opportunity costs before deciding whether to rely on it or add structured selection and promotion mechanisms.

## Approach

1. Use the completed emergent-path item (`2026-11-07-emergent-friction-lodging`) to identify the capabilities and safeguards available without a dedicated pipeline.
2. Use the completed dedicated-pipeline item (`2026-11-07-dedicated-innovation-pipeline-value`) to identify the incremental mechanisms and benefits that a formal pipeline may provide.
3. Compare the approaches on four explicit dimensions: problem coverage, speed of learning, quality of selection, and ability to scale improvements.
4. For each claimed loss, distinguish whether it is directly evidenced, inferred from a missing mechanism, or dependent on organisational context.
5. Identify countervailing strengths of emergent lodging and conditions where the absence of a pipeline creates little or no material loss.
6. Produce findings that can feed the subsequent cross-item synthesis into an operating standard.

### §0 Initialise

Question: Compared with a dedicated innovation pipeline (a funded, staffed process that collects, selects, resources, and promotes improvement ideas), what is lost in problem coverage, speed of learning, selection quality, and ability to scale when an organisation relies only on emergent problem lodging (teams recording friction in a shared list without a formal budget)?

Scope: gaps on four dimensions; mitigating and amplifying conditions; countervailing strengths of emergent lodging.

Constraints: evidence-based losses are separated from losses inferred from a missing mechanism; missing capability is separated from measured performance impact.

Prior-work check: the two prerequisite repository items supply the evidence base. The emergent-path item is at https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-11-07-emergent-friction-lodging.html and the pipeline item is at https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-11-07-dedicated-innovation-pipeline-value.html. Adjacent items on local-global throughput, fragmentation, shadow Information Technology (IT) governance, and platform engineering qualify the scaling dimension. [assumption; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-11-07-dedicated-innovation-pipeline-value.html]

Working hypothesis: most losses are losses of a mechanism (sponsor, funding protection, termination rule, shared experiment tooling, authority to scale), not measured performance deficits, because the prerequisite items found no head-to-head comparison. ### §2 Investigation

#### Q1 Problem coverage

Emergent lodging depends on people being willing to raise problems, and the available support for that is indirect. [inference; source: https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/13a7b031-0fdd-45ec-a7e0-2b80e2bc679f/full] Edmondson (1999) associated team psychological safety with learning behavior in 51 manufacturing teams, and the study measured learning behavior rather than the raising of problems. [fact; source: https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/13a7b031-0fdd-45ec-a7e0-2b80e2bc679f/full]

A randomised field experiment with 19 teams found that rewards for approved ideas broadened participation, and the broader base persisted after the reward stopped. [fact; source: https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/8096] An unfunded emergent register offers no reward channel of this kind, so participation breadth is a possible loss. [inference; source: https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/8096]

Local gains can degrade whole-system throughput when a shared downstream queue is not expanded, so a problem that only shows up across teams is invisible to a register read team by team. [inference; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-06-13-local-global-optima-knowledge-work-throughput.html] Cross-unit visibility was also the setting where IBM's matrix structure blocked emergent ideas before its Emerging Business Opportunity (EBO) system. [inference; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf]

#### Q2 Speed of learning

Fewer than a third of ideas moved their target metrics when tested at Bing, so cheap testing and retirement matter more to learning speed than approval. [inference; source: https://blogs.bing.com/search-quality-insights/2013/8/Large-Scale-Experimentation-at-Bing/] Bing teams ran over 10,000 experiments per year autonomously on a shared platform, so throughput came from platform investment and federated ownership rather than central approval. [inference; source: https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2017-05%20ICSE2017_EvolutionOfExP.pdf]

An unfunded emergent register therefore does not lose speed because it lacks an approval layer, and it can lose speed if it lacks shared experiment tooling that a funded body would build. [inference; source: https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2017-05%20ICSE2017_EvolutionOfExP.pdf]

The emergent-path item located no source measuring how an experiment-tracking mechanism affects experiment volume or adoption. [fact; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-07-20-regulated-safe-to-fail-probing-operating-models.html]

#### Q3 Selection quality

Before the EBO system, IBM lacked disciplines for selecting, funding, and terminating new businesses, and ideas lacking a senior sponsor were often the first cut when budgets tightened. [fact; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf] Sponsor availability therefore shaped selection in that case, which is the unequal-voice mechanism an emergent register inherits. [inference; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf]

IBM's earlier corporate venture fund produced loosely specified proposals, so money without selection criteria did not give a working pipeline. [inference; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf] A budget alone is thus not the missing element.

Shape Up argues that unpruned backlogs accumulate items nobody will address and replaces them with a short betting table where unselected items are dropped and important ones return. [fact; source: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/2.1-chapter-07] Kubernetes triage rules age out inactive issues and assign owners. [fact; source: https://github.com/kubernetes/community/blob/master/contributors/guide/issue-triage.md] These rules show an emergent register can carry selection and retirement rules without a funded pipeline, in software teams. [inference; source: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/2.1-chapter-07; https://github.com/kubernetes/community/blob/master/contributors/guide/issue-triage.md]

A formal pipeline's ex ante selection is itself weak: fewer than a third of tested ideas moved their metrics at Bing. [fact; source: https://blogs.bing.com/search-quality-insights/2013/8/Large-Scale-Experimentation-at-Bing/]

#### Q4 Ability to scale improvements

After 18 EBOs were funded, only two were rated green on winning in the marketplace in early 2003, so the pipeline's early yield was stronger at funding than at scaling. [inference; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf]

A regulated safe-to-fail pilot generated learning but could not scale because its model would exceed existing statutory authority. [fact; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-07-20-regulated-safe-to-fail-probing-operating-models.html] Authority above the experiment can block scaling whatever the pipeline design. [inference; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-07-20-regulated-safe-to-fail-probing-operating-models.html]

Across 35 Xerox spin-offs, a Xerox insider as chief executive correlated with lower performance and venture capital board share with higher performance. [fact; source: https://www.academia.edu/120648458/The_governance_and_performance_of_Xerox_s_technology_spin_off_companies] Governance of the promotion path can therefore reduce the value of promoted ideas, in either approach. [inference; source: https://www.academia.edu/120648458/The_governance_and_performance_of_Xerox_s_technology_spin_off_companies]

Shadow IT literature names continuity lack, where locally owned tooling loses support when its owner leaves, as a recurring risk, and a staged identify, evaluate, and allocate sequence offers a route from covert local solutions to owned governance. [fact; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-06-13-shadow-it-custom-tooling-governance-transition.html] Without a pipeline owner for that sequence, locally lodged improvements may stay unowned. [inference; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-06-13-shadow-it-custom-tooling-governance-transition.html]

Aggregate costs of local tooling can exceed customization benefits when tool footprint, shared dependency density, and staff turnover reach a combined threshold, so unmanaged local scaling has a ceiling. [inference; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-06-13-local-tooling-fragmentation-threshold-measurement.html] A golden path (an opinionated, supported default with permitted deviation) is the most consistently documented pattern for keeping local agility without fragmentation, and it needs a standing owner. [inference; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-06-13-platform-engineering-innersource-hybrid-standardization.html]

#### Q5 Countervailing strengths

Burgelman's Intel study proposes that successful strategic reorientations were preceded by internal experimentation through an autonomous process. [fact; source: https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/intraorganizational-ecology-strategy-making-organizational-adaptation] Emergent action can therefore deliver strategic novelty where a sponsor exists. [inference; source: https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/intraorganizational-ecology-strategy-making-organizational-adaptation]

A pipeline's overhead is most plausibly justified where ideas cross unit boundaries, sponsorship is scarce, and volume is high enough to amortise shared tooling, and least justified for cheap single-team changes. [inference; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf; https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2017-05%20ICSE2017_EvolutionOfExP.pdf]

Search record: no source located compares emergent lodging and a dedicated pipeline within one organisation; the pipeline item searched for this and recorded "not found". [fact; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-11-07-dedicated-innovation-pipeline-value.html]

#### Q6 Conditions

Losses are amplified when ideas cross unit boundaries, when sponsors are scarce, when shared queues are constrained, and when authority to scale sits above the team. [inference; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf; https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-06-13-local-global-optima-knowledge-work-throughput.html] They are mitigated by a short register with ageing rules, a named promoter, and shared experiment tooling. [inference; source: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/2.1-chapter-07; https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2017-05%20ICSE2017_EvolutionOfExP.pdf]

### §3 Reasoning

1. Missing-mechanism losses: cross-unit sponsorship, protected funding, termination discipline, and an owner for promotion are present in a pipeline and absent from a bare register. [inference; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf]
2. Directly evidenced loss: ideas without a senior sponsor were cut first at IBM before its formal system. [fact; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf]
3. Not a loss of a pipeline per se: experiment throughput at Microsoft came from shared tooling. [inference; source: https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2017-05%20ICSE2017_EvolutionOfExP.pdf]
4. Not evidenced: that selection by a pipeline is more accurate than selection by an emergent register. [inference; source: https://blogs.bing.com/search-quality-insights/2013/8/Large-Scale-Experimentation-at-Bing/]
5. Scale-blocking limits above the experiment apply to both approaches. [inference; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-07-20-regulated-safe-to-fail-probing-operating-models.html]

### §4 Consistency Check

The IBM case supports a loss without a pipeline, while the Intel case supports emergent success; the two are reconciled by sponsor availability as the condition separating them. [inference; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf; https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/intraorganizational-ecology-strategy-making-organizational-adaptation]

### §5 Depth and Breadth Expansion

Economic lens: a pipeline adds fixed cost in funding, staff time, and executive attention, so its net value depends on idea volume and cross-unit share. [inference; source: https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2017-05%20ICSE2017_EvolutionOfExP.pdf] The tooling cost is shared across experiments, which favours the pipeline only at higher volume. [inference; source: https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2017-05%20ICSE2017_EvolutionOfExP.pdf]

Behavioural lens: reward-driven participation persisted after rewards ended in one firm, which suggests a funded recognition channel can widen the contributor base, though one firm limits transfer. [inference; source: https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/8096]

Historical lens: the IBM and Intel cases come from large firms in different decades and sectors, so neither generalises on its own. [inference; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf; https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/intraorganizational-ecology-strategy-making-organizational-adaptation]

### §6 Synthesis

**Executive summary:**

Relying only on emergent lodging most plausibly loses cross-unit coverage, a standing sponsor and termination discipline, and an owner for scaling, while it does not by itself lose experiment speed or selection accuracy. [inference; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf; https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2017-05%20ICSE2017_EvolutionOfExP.pdf] The only directly evidenced loss is the IBM finding that unsponsored ideas were cut first. [fact; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf] No source located compares the two approaches head to head. [fact; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-11-07-dedicated-innovation-pipeline-value.html] The losses are conditional on cross-unit ideas, scarce sponsors, and authority above the team. [inference; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-06-13-local-global-optima-knowledge-work-throughput.html]

### Key Findings

1. Before its Emerging Business Opportunity system, IBM lacked disciplines for selecting, funding, and terminating new businesses, and ideas lacking a senior sponsor were often the first cut when budgets tightened, which is the clearest directly evidenced loss. ([fact]; medium confidence; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf)
2. Money without selection criteria did not create a working pipeline at IBM, so the loss without a pipeline is the absence of explicit selection and termination rules rather than the absence of a budget alone. ([inference]; medium confidence; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf)
3. Experiment throughput at Microsoft came from a shared platform with federated ownership, so an emergent organisation loses speed of learning only if it also lacks shared experiment tooling. ([inference]; medium confidence; source: https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2017-05%20ICSE2017_EvolutionOfExP.pdf)
4. Fewer than a third of ideas moved their target metrics in controlled tests at Bing, so a pipeline's ex ante selection is not shown to beat cheap testing and retirement in an emergent system. ([inference]; medium confidence; source: https://blogs.bing.com/search-quality-insights/2013/8/Large-Scale-Experimentation-at-Bing/)
5. Local tooling gains degraded whole-system throughput where a shared downstream queue was not expanded, so a register read team by team can miss cross-team value that a coordinating body would see. ([inference]; medium confidence; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-06-13-local-global-optima-knowledge-work-throughput.html)
6. Rewards for approved ideas broadened participation in a 19-team field experiment and the broader base persisted, so an unfunded register may lose participation breadth, though only one firm was studied. ([inference]; low confidence; source: https://www.iza.org/publications/dp/8096)
7. A regulated safe-to-fail pilot generated learning but could not scale because authority sat above it, so a scaling loss can arise from limits that neither approach controls. ([fact]; medium confidence; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-07-20-regulated-safe-to-fail-probing-operating-models.html)
8. Shadow IT literature names continuity lack as a recurring risk of locally owned tooling and offers a staged identify, evaluate, and allocate route to owned governance, so improvements lodged emergently may stay unowned without a standing promoter. ([inference]; medium confidence; source: https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-06-13-shadow-it-custom-tooling-governance-transition.html; https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-06-13-local-tooling-fragmentation-threshold-measurement.html)
9. Burgelman's Intel study proposes that reorientations were preceded by autonomous internal experimentation, so emergent action can deliver strategic novelty where a sponsor exists and the loss from having no pipeline is then small. ([inference]; medium confidence; source: https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/intraorganizational-ecology-strategy-making-organizational-adaptation)
10. A short register with ageing rules and a named promoter is documented in software teams, so a bare register is not the only emergent form, and some selection loss can be reduced without a funded pipeline. ([inference]; medium confidence; source: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/2.1-chapter-07; https://github.com/kubernetes/community/blob/master/contributors/guide/issue-triage.md)

### Assumptions

The IBM, Intel, and Microsoft cases are assumed to indicate patterns for medium-to-large knowledge-work organisations rather than transferable rates. [assumption; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf]
The assumption is reasonable because no study located reports rates for process-improvement ideas, and each case describes a mechanism rather than a benchmark. [assumption; source: https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2017-05%20ICSE2017_EvolutionOfExP.pdf]
Open-source and software-team register rules are assumed to transfer to employees with competing assigned work. [assumption; source: https://github.com/kubernetes/community/blob/master/contributors/guide/issue-triage.md]
That transfer is reasonable because both settings manage a shared inflow of problem reports with limited reviewer time. [assumption; source: https://basecamp.com/shapeup/2.1-chapter-07]

### Analysis

Losses fall into three classes: directly evidenced (unsponsored ideas cut first), inferred from a missing mechanism (cross-unit visibility, termination rules, promotion owner), and context-dependent (participation breadth, scaling ceilings). [inference; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf; https://davidamitchell.github.io/Research/research/2026-06-13-local-global-optima-knowledge-work-throughput.html]
Speed of learning and selection accuracy show the weakest case for a pipeline, since throughput traced to shared tooling and ex ante selection yielded few winners. [inference; source: https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2017-05%20ICSE2017_EvolutionOfExP.pdf; https://blogs.bing.com/search-quality-insights/2013/8/Large-Scale-Experimentation-at-Bing/]
Sponsorship could instead explain Intel's emergent success and IBM's emergent failure, which means the loss may track sponsor availability rather than pipeline presence. [inference; source: https://www.elkarbide.com/sites/default/files/ibm_ebo_1.pdf; https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/intraorganizational-ecology-strategy-making-organizational-adaptation]
Rival remedies to a full pipeline are investing in shared experiment tooling, adding a named promoter and ageing rules to the register, or redesigning decision rights, and the sources support each at small scale but none was compared with a pipeline.
